# GAS Multifractal Model and Its Connection to the Markov-Switching Multifractal (MSM)

## 1. Model Definition and Dynamics

Consider a univariate return process
$$
y_t = \sigma_t \varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal N(0,1),
$$
where conditional heteroskedasticity is generated through a multifractal volatility cascade.

### 1.1 Multifractal Volatility Grid

Let $k \in \mathbb N$ denote the number of cascade components. Define a binomial volatility grid
$$
\sigma^2(j) = \exp(\bar\psi)\, m_1^{\,k-j} m_0^{\,j}, \qquad j=0,\dots,k,
$$
with $m_0 \in (0,2)$, $m_1 = 2 - m_0$, and $\bar\psi \in \mathbb R$. This grid coincides with the standard binomial MSM volatility support.

### 1.2 Probability-Space Representation

Introduce latent Bernoulli indicators $z_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$, $i=1,\dots,k$, and define
$$
J_t = \sum_{i=1}^k z_{i,t}.
$$
Conditional on the vector of probabilities $p_t = (p_{1,t},\dots,p_{k,t})$, the distribution of $J_t$ is Poisson–binomial with weights
$$
w_t(j) = \Pr(J_t=j \mid p_t), \qquad j=0,\dots,k.
$$

The observation density is the finite Gaussian mixture
$$
p(y_t \mid p_t)
= \sum_{j=0}^k w_t(j)\,\varphi\!\left(y_t;0,\sigma^2(j)\right),
$$
where $\varphi(\cdot;0,v)$ denotes the $\mathcal N(0,v)$ density.

### 1.3 Score-Driven Dynamics

Define probabilities through a logistic link
$$
p_{i,t} = \ell(\tau f_{i,t}), \qquad \ell(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}, \quad \tau>0,
$$
and let the latent state $f_t \in \mathbb R^k$ evolve according to
$$
f_{t+1} = \Phi f_t + \alpha s_t,
$$
where $\Phi = \mathrm{diag}(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_k)$, $\alpha \in \mathbb R$, and
$$
s_t = \nabla_{f_t} \log p(y_t \mid p_t)
$$
is the exact score of the mixture likelihood.

## 2. Likelihood

For a sample $\{y_t\}_{t=1}^T$, the conditional log-likelihood is
$$
\mathcal L_T(\theta)
= \sum_{t=1}^T \log\!\left[
\sum_{j=0}^k w_t(j)\,(2\pi\sigma^2(j))^{-1/2}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{y_t^2}{2\sigma^2(j)}\right)
\right],
$$
where $\theta$ collects structural parameters and the initial state $f_1$.

---

## 3. Stationarity Conditions

The state recursion can be written as a stochastic recurrence equation
$$
f_{t+1} = \Phi f_t + \alpha s(f_t,y_t).
$$

A sufficient condition for strict stationarity and ergodicity is contraction on average:
$$
\mathbb E\!\left[\log \left\|\Phi + \alpha J_s(f_t,y_t)\right\|\right] < 0,
$$
where $J_s = \partial s / \partial f^\top$ is the Jacobian of the score map. This condition parallels standard results for GAS models and ensures existence and uniqueness of a stationary solution.

## 4. Connection with the Markov-Switching Multifractal Model

### 4.1 Classical MSM

In the binomial MSM,
$$
\sigma_t^2 = \bar\sigma^2 \prod_{i=1}^k M_{i,t},
$$
where each $M_{i,t} \in \{m_0,m_1\}$ follows an independent two-state Markov chain:
$$
M_{i,t} =
\begin{cases}
\text{draw from } \{m_0,m_1\} & \text{with prob. } \gamma_i,\\
M_{i,t-1} & \text{with prob. } 1-\gamma_i.
\end{cases}
$$
The switching probabilities $\gamma_i$ obey a geometric hierarchy
$$
\gamma_i = 1-(1-\gamma_1)b^{-i}, \qquad b>1.
$$

### 4.2 Probability Dynamics in MSM

Let $z_{i,t} = \mathbf 1\{M_{i,t}=m_0\}$ and define
$$
p^{\mathrm{MSM}}_{i,t} = \Pr(z_{i,t}=1 \mid \mathcal F_{t-1}).
$$
Then
$$
p^{\mathrm{MSM}}_{i,t}
= (1-\gamma_i)\,p^{\mathrm{MSM}}_{i,t-1} + \frac{\gamma_i}{2},
$$
so deviations $\delta_{i,t}=p^{\mathrm{MSM}}_{i,t}-\tfrac12$ satisfy
$$
\delta_{i,t} = (1-\gamma_i)\,\delta_{i,t-1}.
$$

### 4.3 Mapping to the GAS Dynamics

Linearizing the logistic link around $p_{i,t}=1/2$ yields
$$
p_{i,t}-\tfrac12 \approx \frac{\tau}{4} f_{i,t}.
$$
If the score term has zero mean, the leading-order dynamics imply
$$
p_{i,t+1}-\tfrac12 \approx \phi_i\,(p_{i,t}-\tfrac12).
$$

Thus, setting
$$
\phi_i = 1-\gamma_i
$$
aligns the persistence of the GAS probability dynamics with the MSM switching structure.

## 5. Moment Scaling Equivalence

### 5.1 MSM Autocovariance Structure

In MSM,
$$
\log\sigma_t^2 = \bar\psi + \sum_{i=1}^k \log M_{i,t},
$$
and independence across $i$ implies
$$
\mathrm{Cov}(\log\sigma_t^2,\log\sigma_{t+h}^2)
= \sum_{i=1}^k c_i (1-\gamma_i)^h,
$$
with $c_i = \mathrm{Var}(\log M_i)$.

This mixture of exponentials, combined with the geometric spacing of $\gamma_i$, generates power-law decay and multifractal moment scaling.

### 5.2 GAS Autocovariance Structure

Under the linearization,
$$
\log\sigma_t^2 - \mathbb E\log\sigma^2
\approx \sum_{i=1}^k a_i f_{i,t},
$$
for constants $a_i$. If the score innovations are martingale differences,
$$
\mathrm{Cov}(f_{i,t},f_{i,t+h}) \propto \phi_i^h,
$$
so
$$
\mathrm{Cov}(\log\sigma_t^2,\log\sigma_{t+h}^2)
\approx \sum_{i=1}^k d_i \phi_i^h.
$$

### 5.3 Equivalence Result

If
$$
\phi_i = 1-\gamma_i \quad \text{for all } i,
$$
then the autocovariance kernels coincide up to scale. Since the scaling of aggregated return moments
$$
\mathbb E|Y_\tau|^q, \qquad Y_\tau=\sum_{s=1}^\tau y_{t+s},
$$
depends only on this kernel, both models imply identical scaling exponents $H(q)$.

## 6. Advantages and Disadvantages Relative to MSM

### Advantages
- **Dimensionality reduction**: the state dimension is $k$ rather than $2^k$.
- **Deterministic filtering**: no latent-state simulation or particle filtering is required.
- **Exact likelihood**: the observation density matches the MSM mixture exactly.
- **Scalability**: computational cost grows polynomially in $k$.

### Disadvantages
- **Approximate equivalence**: exact MSM dynamics are recovered only under linearization or specific parameter restrictions.
- **Loss of exact Bayesian updating**: regime probabilities evolve via score feedback rather than a full HMM filter.
- **Dependence structure**: posterior dependence across components present in MSM is replaced by conditional independence.

---

## 7. Concluding Statement

The score-driven multifractal model provides a probability-space representation of the binomial MSM that preserves the multifractal volatility cascade and its moment-scaling properties under appropriate parameter mapping. By replacing the hidden Markov filter with a deterministic score recursion, the model achieves substantial computational gains while retaining the core multifractal structure responsible for long-memory and scaling behavior.
